## 1. Intent

Plan 9a-1 gave people a plain board page: the state, the stop entry, the conclusion and the ranked hypotheses, read once and never refreshed. Plan 9a-2 makes it the board spec Section 10.2 describes. A responder sees what the agents found and why, as it commits; the evidence behind each hypothesis; what the agents suggest doing; what failed along the way; and how this item relates to the others of its incident. They see it on a phone first, and beside the feed on a wide screen. Plans 7c-1a and 7c-1b give this board its content: items opened by alerts, alerts that resolve, flap and come back late, and incidents that join items.

**Goals**
- **The header**, generated by rules and refreshed as entries commit:
  - the state and the stop reason;
  - the conclusion with its status word, `confirming` included;
  - the budget used;
  - the number of suggestions;
  - the alert's instances and firing state;
  - "last updated" as the time of the item's latest record or board entry.
- **The incident strip**: the other items of the incident, each with its state and conclusion, flagged when they disagree.
- **The hypotheses**: ranked, each with up to [2] quotes per side by the spec's fixed rule and a count of the rest, and its suggestions beneath it. The ruled-out hypotheses follow.
- **The feed**: chronological, merging the board's entries with the item's records, with authors and provenance links, last and collapsed on a phone.
- **Notices shown to people.**
- **The full board-state table** of spec 10.2, rows 1 to 11 and 14.
- **Live refresh, the connection indicator, and one polite summary per round** for screen readers.
- **The wide layout.**
- **Raw outputs**, for the item's responders.
- **The bidi and hidden-character carry**, one display rule for stored text.

**Non-goals (other rows)**
- Rulings after the stop entry, human evidence, messages, milestones by hand, the close-out form, adoption answers, notifications, and accessibility tested with assistive technology: 9b.
- The degraded and diagnosis-only banners (rows 12 and 13): no item field holds them until 7c-2.
- The rater role and its raw-output access: 8b.
- Hiding invisible-arm boards: 8a. This plan states what every new route does for an item the `visible` seam hides (D11, D13).

**Anti-goals**
- Any text from an agent, a person, a source or a tool reaching a page as markup, reordering the platform's words around it, or reading as the platform's own words.
- A page that only works with JavaScript.
- Viewers of a busy board taking the database from the coordinator.
- Focus or a reader's place lost while they read.
- Raw outputs shown to anyone the spec does not name.

## 2. Decisions

### D1. Live refresh: conditional polling, with server rendering kept (review I1, I2; out-of-frame A)

- **Polling, not SSE (the designer's call).** Spec 10.2 asks for a header "refreshed as entries commit" and an indicator reading "live" or "reconnecting, last updated at T". It names no transport; only roadmap row 9a-2 says "SSE". EventSource fails for good on any non-200 answer, such as a deploy's 502, never reads `retry:` on one, cannot see comment heartbeats, loops through sign-in when the session expires, and holds one of a browser's six HTTP/1.1 connections per tab (the review's I1 and I2). Conditional polling has none of those failure modes, at the cost of up to [5 s] of lag on rounds of 150 s. This changes the roadmap row's word, which the author's approval of this design rules.
- **The script.** The board page gains one same-origin static script, `/static/live.<hash>.js`, with no inline script and no `eval`. Its URL carries a build hash, so a deploy never runs a stale script.
  - **Without the script**, the page is the full 9a-2 board, plus "This page does not refresh itself: reload it to see more". The script is a progressive enhancement, never a dependency.
  - **With it**, the page polls `GET /items/{id}/board` every [5 s] while the tab is visible, with `If-None-Match` set to the `ETag` of what it shows.
- **The answer (re-review N1).** Every poll renders the board's regions, the same server render as the page without the layout. It renders from the cached projection (D2) plus the facts computed at each render, so a poll costs no database read unless the version changed. The `ETag` is a hash of the rendered regions. The server answers `304` when it equals `If-None-Match`, and otherwise `200` with the regions. So whatever the page shows reaches it within one poll of changing: the strip, the queue position, "N min ago", the kill switch.
- **The regions.** The regions are:
  - the fixed kill-switch header and the standby notice (review I3);
  - the board's header;
  - the strip;
  - the hypotheses;
  - the feed;
  - the round summary.

  Each carries a hash of its own content, so the script replaces only the regions that changed (re-review, I5).
- **No HTML sink (review M1).** The script fetches with `XMLHttpRequest`, `responseType = "document"`, which yields an inert parsed document, and moves the changed regions in with `replaceWith(document.adoptNode(…))`. Board pages send `script-src 'self'; connect-src 'self'; require-trusted-types-for 'script'; trusted-types 'none'`, added to 9a-1's policy. No Trusted Types policy can then exist, and no string ever reaches an HTML sink. Every other page keeps 9a-1's exact policy, and a new test pins the board pages' policy.
- **Order (review M9).**
  - **In flight:** one fetch at a time, with a dirty flag that fetches again when it returns.
  - **Stale responses (re-review N3):** a visible item's page and every response carry the order of what they show, the item's latest record seq and the board's latest entry seq, as `data-order` and an `X-Board-Order` header. The script drops a response whose order is below the one it applied, whichever instance served it. A person who can see the board sees every entry anyway, so these seqs tell them nothing new. A hidden item carries no order (D13).
  - **The query:** the fetch carries the page's query, so `?feed=all` survives a refresh.
  - **The first poll:** the page is rendered with its `ETag`, so the first poll answers `304`.
- **Focus (review I5).**
  - **The region in use:** a region holding `document.activeElement` is never replaced. It is marked stale ("New entries: update"), and replaced when the focus leaves it or the person asks.
  - **Everything else:** other regions are replaced only when their own version changed. Focus is restored by id when the element survives.
  - **Pausing (re-review I5):** the header holds a "Pause updates" button (`aria-pressed`). While paused, every changed region but the header shows "New entries: update" rather than changing, so a screen reader in browse mode keeps its place. Accessibility tested with assistive technology is 9b's.
  - **What a replacement keeps (re-review N6):** each `<details>` element's `open` state, matched by id, and the scroll position, so a phone's feed does not reopen on every update.
  - **What the spec requires:** spec 10.2 asks only the header to refresh as entries commit, which it always does unless focus sits inside it.
- **The indicator.**
  - **"live"** while the last poll succeeded within twice the interval.
  - **"reconnecting, last updated at T"** otherwise, with T rendered by the server: the latest record or board entry time.
  - **Backoff:** the script backs off on errors, doubling to [60 s]. It pauses while the tab is hidden, and polls at once on `visibilitychange` and `pageshow`.
- **Sign-in (review I1, re-review M8).** `/items/{id}/board` never redirects. A request without a live session gets `401` with no body, and the script then reloads the page, so sign-in only ever begins from a top-level navigation. The script reloads at most once per [60 s]; a second `401` inside that minute shows "Sign in again" as a link instead, so a key rotation cannot loop it. `Available`'s `503` and a proxy's errors are only errors: the script backs off.
- **The polite summary.** The server renders one sentence per round into the summary region, such as "Round 3 ended: leading, *key*". The script copies it into an `aria-live="polite"` region only when the round number changes, so a screen reader hears one summary per round, not one per entry (spec 10.4).
- **The phone's feed (review M2).** The feed renders as `<details open>`, and the script closes it below [60em]. Without the script a phone sees the feed expanded, and it is last on the page anyway.

### D2. The version and the load (review I3, I4, I10)

- **The version is internal.** It keys the projection cache and is never sent: the page sees only the `ETag`, a hash of what it renders (D1), and, for a visible item, the order of D1. It is computed from:
  - the id and seq of the item's latest record;
  - the id and seq of the visible run's latest board entry.

  Ids are UUIDs, so a database restore that reuses seqs cannot alias two boards. The kill switch, the lead state, the queue position and the strip are facts computed at each render, not parts of the version.
- **For an item the `visible` seam hides,** the regions render its hidden copy and state word only, so the `ETag` changes only with that word, the kill-switch header and the standby notice. No seq or time of its run reaches the page (review I10, re-review N5).
- **The probe.** One query per instance per [1 s] tick serves every item polled in the last [10 s]: `max(seq)` and the id of the latest row per item over `work_records` (key `tenant_id, item_id, seq`), and the same per run over `board_events` (key `tenant_id, run_id, seq`). Each poll reads the result of the latest tick, so the database sees one probe per second whatever the number of viewers. The page itself is rendered from the same tick's result, never from a fresher read, so a poll cannot show less than the page did (re-review N3). This holds just after a person's own act too: the act's own answer says what happened, and a live board shows it at its next poll. A poll for an item the last tick did not cover probes that item once directly, then joins the tick (re-review N7). The kill switch's latest row comes from the coordinator's own view on the leader and from one shared read per tick on a standby.
- **Routing to one instance (re-review N4).** The example deployment profile routes the web, like the admin API, to the instance `/leaderz` names, so a person's polls reach one instance and the standby notice does not flip between polls. A takeover is one move to the next leader.
- **What is cached.** The projection is cached by version, at most [64] versions per instance, and built once per version (singleflight). It holds only what the records and the board determine. The rest is computed at each render from memory or a shared read:
  - the queue position;
  - facts that depend on the kill switch, such as the auto-close's pause;
  - the standby notice;
  - the viewer's act buttons and raw-output links.
- **The strip from records (review I4).** A sibling's entry is built from its records alone: its state, the `RunStopped` conclusion, and one read of that hypothesis's board entry, by `board_events`' `UNIQUE (tenant_id, id)`. No sibling's board is replayed. The strip is cached per incident for [30 s] and shared by every viewer. A sibling's own failure shows as "this item could not be read".
- **The web's own pool (re-review I4).** Every database read the web makes goes through a pool of its own of [2] connections: pages, projections, siblings, raw outputs and the probe. The coordinator's main pool, which defaults to `max(4, NumCPU)`, is never touched by a viewer. A poll that gets no connection within [2 s] answers `503`, which the script backs off from. The web's stores are built in serve with the web pool, read-only through `app.Views`. Projections are also built once per version (singleflight).
- **Logging.** `/items/{id}/board` logs at Debug, as the health routes do.

### D3. One projection for the board (review M5)

- **The read.** `app.Views` gains `Board(ctx, id) (BoardView, error)`, built from a version the web's probe gives (D2). `Item` is unchanged, so the admin API's C4 is unchanged; the two share an internal builder.
- **What `BoardView` holds:**
  - `Header`: the state, the stop reason, the conclusion and its status, the budget used, the suggestion count, the alert (each instance with its firing state), the latest time, the deadlines of D5, and the flap origin;
  - `Strip` (D2, D7);
  - `Hypotheses`: ranked, each with its quotes (D4) and suggestions (D6), the ruled-out ones apart;
  - `Feed` (D8);
  - `Notices` (D9).
- **Trust.** Every part comes from the records and the board, and every time in it is a database time.
- **Visibility.** One predicate decides it, used by the page and the strip alike. `app.Views` takes it as an option, all visible until 8a. A hidden sibling shows its state only, and its board is never read.
- **Board reads.** New reads in `internal/work/board`, which stays pure: `State.Quotes(h, perSide)`; `State.Proposals(h)`; `State.Events()`, a copy for the feed; and the status words of D5.

### D4. The quote rule (review I7)

For each hypothesis and each side, supports and refutes:

1. **The candidates are the entries behind each counted record of spec 4.3's assessment.**
   - **Supports:** every live supporting record of each contributor whose latest stance supports. For an evidence record, that is its own entry. For the hypothesis author's record, it is each cited evidence entry that counted (`assess.go`'s author record).
   - **Refutes:** the latest record of each refuting contributor.
   - **Aliases:** included.
   - **Sharing the walk:** `stanceRecord` gains its backing entry ids, so `Quotes` and the assessment share one walk, with no copy of the computation. The score and the quotes therefore never disagree.
2. **The order.** Platform evidence first, then human, then agent. Within a class, newest first, by the evidence entry's board seq.
3. **The pick.** Walk that order and take the first entry of each distinct source until [2] are taken. "And N more" counts the remaining candidates.
4. **What a quote shows.**
   - **An agent's evidence:** its verbatim quote, a platform fact, with its source and a link to the raw output (D10).
   - **Human evidence:** the person's claim, as a person's words, with their name.
   - **Platform evidence:** shown from its typed record, never from the stored claim string (D8, review I8).

### D5. Status words and the header's times (review I6, M4)

- **The status words.** `board.StatusOf` gains `confirming`: a converged hypothesis while the run's latest round is a wrap-up round for it and the run has not stopped. A suggestion is `superseded` when its hypothesis is rejected now, or is not converged now and was converged at some round's end. The second case reads `RoundEnded.Converged` from the item's records, with no replay. That is spec 6.3's "lost convergence", read as written.
- **"Confirming until T".** T is the wrap-up round's recorded `RoundOpened.Deadline` plus `Grace` and `SettleWait`, since the decision lands up to that much later.
- **"By T at the latest"**, in the investigating row. T is computed forward from the latest records, never from the run's start:

  **T = D + W + max(0, B − U) + n·W + k·(L + W)**

  - D is the open round's recorded deadline, or the latest record's time between rounds;
  - W is `Grace` + `SettleWait` + one retry wait;
  - B is the wall-clock budget with every extension, and U is the counted wall clock, with the open round taken to D only when it is a normal round (a wrap-up round counts no wall clock);
  - n is the number of normal rounds that can still open;
  - k is the number of counted wrap-up rounds left, less the open one only if it counts (re-review);
  - L is the run's `RoundLimit`.

  The plan pins the formula against `item/decide.go`'s stop rules and `rounds.go`'s waits. The test drives the coordinator's real rounds, end waits included, through both kinds of resume, and asserts that the stop never comes after the T shown. An outage, a takeover or a resume moves T later, and the page shows the new T: the copy says so.
- **"Last updated".** It is the later of the item's latest record time and the visible board's latest entry time (9a-1's carry).

### D6. Suggestions (review M3)

- **Which proposals.** The visible primary run's recorded proposals, under the hypothesis each cites, grouped by target (spec 6.3).
- **What each shows:**
  - **The summary**, from its `SummaryPart`s: text parts as the platform's words, value parts as quoted data (the 5b carry).
  - **The facts:** each name, with its values as quoted data, "and more", or "unavailable".
  - **The live status** of the cited hypothesis.
  - **The proposer and how its support was obtained:** the proposer's own counted evidence on that hypothesis, as a count of each source.
  - **The superseded mark** of D5. Adoption and its warning are 9b's.
- **The service mark.** It is decided from the locked revision's tool policy. A suggestion is marked "outside this item's service" when a scoped argument whose binding is `hypothesis.service` (spec 3.3's example) got a value other than the item's `Routing["service"]`. Where neither side can be determined, the suggestion reads "service not compared", never nothing, so the mark cannot fail silently. (The designer's call.)
- **The count.** The header counts every suggestion, as spec 10.2 asks in Phase 0.

### D7. The incident strip (review M5)

- **When it shows.** Only when the incident has other items.
- **What each item shows.** Its state, and its conclusion's key with the conclusion's status word, built from records as D2 says.
- **Disagreement.** The strip flags "disagree" when two visible siblings of the same work item type both have converged conclusions with different keys. Keys of different types are not comparable.
- **Hidden siblings** show their state only (7c-1a's M13), and never count towards the flag.
- **The cap.** At most [10] other items are shown, the newest first, with "and N more".
- **A failed read.** When the incident's items cannot be listed, the strip says so, and the rest of the board renders. This is 7c-1a's `IncidentItemsUnread` carry: today such a failure fails the whole view. A sibling's own failure is its own entry.

### D8. The feed (review I8, M6)

- **The merge.** The visible run's board entries and the item's records, merged by database time. At equal time, records come first, then each store's own seq. Both stores stamp `greatest(clock_timestamp(), previous)` from one database clock.
- **The default view.** The newest [50] are shown, then "show earlier" (`?feed=all`).
- **What the records add**, in the platform's words:
  - the opening, by whom, or by which source;
  - the start, and each run's creation;
  - promotions, closes and reopens, each with by whom and the reason code;
  - extensions;
  - a firing resume, shown as the divider "Reopened after a flap: the alert fired again at T" (row 10);
  - a declined firing resume: "The alert fired again; the agents did not resume:", followed by the reason for each of its seven codes;
  - links and unlinks;
  - each `item_triggered` event, applied ("Alert firing" or "Alert resolved", with its instance and time) or late ("Received late, not applied", row 11).

  A flap item's header names the item it followed.
- **What the board adds:**
  - evidence, with its author, its claim (model-written, a person's or the platform's), its quote, and its provenance link: to the raw output (D10), or for `trigger:<event id>` and `item:<id>` to that record's line in the feed;
  - hypotheses and retractions;
  - rulings (reject, unreject, dismiss);
  - tasks, claims and completions;
  - proposals, by their summary;
  - notices (D9);
  - rounds, as dividers;
  - the stop entry and resumes.

  Turn ends are left out; their effect shows in the round's divider.
- **Platform evidence from typed records (review I8).** A title can read as platform words ("x; the trigger is resolved") or as a routing member ("disk full; service=payments"), so the stored claim string is never shown as the platform's.
  - **The opening, `item:<id>`:** "Opened by \<source\>", then the title as quoted data, then the routing as platform facts, all from the `item_opened` record.
  - **A trigger update, `trigger:<event id>`:** the status, source, instance, time, late flag and resolved flag as the platform's words, from the `item_triggered` record with that source and event id (an event id is unique per source only), then the alert's title as quoted data.

  The title is read from the inbound event store, which already keeps it redacted (`inbound_events.title`), through a new batch read `Inbound.Titles(source, eventIDs)`. No source text is added to the work records, which are chained and kept longer (re-review N7). After the store's retention [30 days], the title reads "no longer kept".
- **Model text.** Every model-written text is marked "model-written" (spec 10.2).
- **Row 8, awaiting close.** It reads "Stays open while its alert fires" when the alert fires, and otherwise names the auto-close time.

### D9. Notices shown to people (review M6)

- **What shows.** Every notice on the visible board, each agent's included, unlike an agent's own view. They show in the feed, in the platform's words for each code. The words cover `refused_call`, `failed_read`, `model_refused`, `model_failed`, `model_unknown`, `no_agents`, `turn_unstarted` (engine, image or launch), `skill_yanked` and `kill_switch`.
- **Repeats.** Repeats in one round with the same code, agent, tool, rule and skill show once, with a count.
- **The header.** It counts the current round's notices. A notice holds names, ids and codes only (spec 4.2), so its words are the platform's.

### D10. Raw outputs (review M7)

- **The route.** `GET /items/{id}/calls/{callID}` shows a read call's stored, redacted output and arguments (`app.ReadCalls`, new to `web.Ports`).
- **Who.** Only the item's principal and responders see it: `May`'s grounds must be `Member`, never `AdminOverride`, which `May` also grants a fresh admin (spec 10.1: "raw outputs are visible only to the item's responders and raters"; raters are 8b). Admins are not added. Everyone else gets `404`, and no link to it is rendered. Before 7c-2 an inbound item has neither a principal nor responders, so no one sees its raw outputs from the web. (The designer's call: no deviation from the spec. Cost: until 7c-2, an alert item's raw outputs are reachable only from the database.)
- **Which calls.** Only a call cited by evidence on the item's visible board, with `ReadCall.Run` checked as well. Any other id is `404`. An item the `visible` seam hides answers `404` for every call.
- **How it renders:**
  - JSON output in its decoded, indented form, the form quotes are matched against (`board/quote.go`), with the quote's line marked;
  - line numbers, wrapped, scrolling only inside its own block (spec 10.4);
  - the first [256 KiB], then "truncated";
  - D11's rule on every line;
  - a call past its retention [30 days] says so.

### D11. One display rule for stored text (review I9)

- **The function.** `inert` returns a plain string, never `template.HTML`, so `html/template` still escapes everything. It replaces each rune of a set with a visible marker such as `⟨U+202E⟩`.
  - **Multi-line text** uses `config.Hidden`: claims, statements, quotes, raw-output lines and titles. Line feeds and tabs stay.
  - **Single-line values** use `config.Unseen`: keys, routing, names, summary values and fact values.
- **Isolation.** The templates hold it, `<bdi>{{inert .X}}</bdi>`, so a text's direction cannot reorder the platform's words around it.
- **Where.** Every board page and the items list. A test renders `<script>` through every call site and finds it escaped.
- **Why.** This is the bidi carry (Plan 1:4964). Agent text and quotes are already refused at post when they hold hidden runes (spec 9.4), and alert titles are already cleaned at mapping. So `inert` is defence in depth everywhere except raw outputs, where it is needed.

### D12. Layout

- **Phones first.** One column, phone portrait first: the header, the strip, the hypotheses, the ruled-out ones, then the feed, last.
- **Wide screens.** From [60em], the feed sits beside the hypotheses (spec 10.4), open (D1).
- **Kept from 9a-1:** 44-point targets, the dark theme, 200% text, and status never shown by color alone.

### D13. Hidden items (review I10, latent until 8a)

For an item the `visible` seam hides:
- the page and `/items/{id}/board` serve 9a-1's hidden copy, with the kill-switch header and the standby notice still refreshed;
- the `ETag` follows the hidden copy alone, no order is sent, and "last updated at T" is left out, since T would be a time of its run (D2);
- raw outputs answer `404` (D10);
- the strip shows it by state only (D7).

No seq, time or nudge of its run reaches anyone. Carried to 8a as a constraint.

### D14. Plan 9a-1's carries that are not the board (review, carried constraints)

- **Taken here:**
  - refuse a subject with surrounding spaces at sign-in;
  - the standby page judges the lease's expiry on the database-time estimate (`web.Ports.Clock` becomes the `clock.DB` estimate; today `time.Now`);
  - a retry after a lost answer and a new sign-in must not open a second item (9a-1's m3; the review calls it a correctness bug, not hygiene). The form key's MAC already binds only the subject, the act, the item and the key (`keys.go:138-144`). The blocker is the CSRF token, bound to the session's `IssuedAt` (`keys.go:135`) and minted anew at each sign-in (`flow.go:337`): the old form is refused with "Reload the page", and the reload brings a new key (re-review N2). So a form refused for its CSRF token that carries a valid form key is rendered again with the same key and MAC under the new token, saying "Your sign-in changed: check and send again". The retry then carries the original key, and the coordinator answers it as a repeat.
- **Carried to 9b, whose act forms it belongs to:** the act forms' previews, which have no time bound and no single flight.

(The designer's call.)

### D15. Tests and gates (review M10)

- **Board domain.** The quote rule, table-driven over classes, sources, sides, retractions, aliases, the author's cited support, and the count. `confirming` and `superseded`.
- **The app.**
  - `Board`'s projection;
  - D5's deadlines, against the coordinator's real rounds;
  - the merge order;
  - the strip: the cap, hidden siblings, disagreement only within a type, a sibling's failure and a failed list;
  - the version's inputs, and the probe in both stores.
- **The pages.**
  - every board-state row by its text, as a table;
  - the no-JS page;
  - `inert` at every call site;
  - platform evidence never shown from its claim string;
  - the raw-output gate, with its member, viewer, admin, uncited-id, other-run and hidden-item cases;
  - the board pages' CSP, and every other page's unchanged CSP;
  - `/board`'s `304`, `200` and `401` without a redirect, an `ETag` that changes when only the strip, the queue position, the minute or the kill switch changes, and `X-Board-Order` on visible items only;
  - the web pool's `503` past its wait, and a wiring test that no web read uses the coordinator's pool;
  - a form refused for its CSRF token after a new sign-in, rendered again with its original key, whose retry the coordinator answers as a repeat.
- **The script.** It stays under about 120 lines, and holds no logic the server could hold.
- **A browser gate at the branch's end,** recorded in the plan: a manual check in Chromium on a desktop and Safari on an iPhone of:
  - opening a board;
  - idling 2 minutes;
  - an instance restart;
  - a phone that sleeps;
  - an expired session;
  - focus inside the feed while entries land;
  - seven tabs;
  - no Trusted Types violation in the console.
- **E2e scenarios.**
  - 33: an inbound item's board, its alert firing then resolved, a late event, and a flap divider; a poll answers `304`, then `200` with a new entry.

## 3. Rulings this version makes (cost if wrong)

1. **Conditional polling instead of SSE** (D1), changing roadmap row 9a-2's word. *Cost:* up to 5 s of lag, and a poll every 5 s per open board, each rendering from a cached projection; the database sees one probe per second per instance.
2. **The web reads through its own pool of [2] connections** (D2). *Cost:* a busy board's viewers wait on 2 connections, answered `503` past 2 s, rather than ever slowing the coordinator.
3. **A "Pause updates" button, and a region holding focus marked stale** (D1). *Cost:* readers who pause see "New entries: update" rather than the entries.
4. **A same-origin script with no HTML sink** (D1): an inert XHR document and `trusted-types 'none'`. *Cost:* a script surface where 9a-1 had none, bounded by the board-only CSP. A browser without XHR documents falls back to reload-only.
5. **The strip from records, cached for 30 s per incident** (D2, D7). *Cost:* a sibling's change shows up to 30 s late, and its conclusion only once its run stops.
6. **The service mark from the locked tool policy's `hypothesis.service` binding, else "service not compared"** (D6). *Cost:* a tool that binds the service through another expression shows "not compared".
7. **Raw outputs only for cited calls, and only to the item's principal and responders; no admins** (D10). *Cost:* until 7c-2, an alert item's raw outputs are not on the web.
8. **The alert's title is read from the inbound event store, not added to the work records** (D8). *Cost:* past the store's 30 days, a trigger update's title reads "no longer kept".
9. **9a-1's duplicate-item carry is taken by re-rendering a CSRF-refused form with its original key; the previews' carry goes to 9b** (D14). *Cost:* about half a day here; previews stay unbounded one plan longer.
10. **Rows 12 and 13 wait for 7c-2's fields** (non-goals). *Cost:* none until 7c-2 adds degraded and diagnosis-only items.
11. **"Lost convergence" read as written** (D5): converged at any round's end, not converged now. *Cost:* a suggestion made after a hypothesis lost convergence also reads superseded.
